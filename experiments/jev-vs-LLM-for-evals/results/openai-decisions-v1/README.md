# OpenAI Decisions judging aggregates

## TL;DR

No judge dominated every benchmark. Qwen3p8 Max led the three headline LLMBar, JudgeBench, and RewardBench 2 methods at 92.53%, 88.39%, and 81.57% strict agreement. Among decision models, SubQ and Jev 1.14 were strongest: SubQ scored 88.06%, 72.10%, and 69.39%, and Jev 1.14 scored 88.24%, 70.81%, and 69.14%, both above Jev 1.13's 87.36%, 69.03%, and 68.12%. Jev 1.14's point estimate exceeded Jev 1.13 on all 19 primary methods, SubQ's on 18 of 19. Decisions was competitive on some LLMBar rating prompts but fell to 23.87% on JudgeBench vanilla because it selected the first-shown response in both presentation orders for 71.1% of pairs. SubQ showed a much smaller 9.2% version of that bias and Jev 1.14 2.9%; both completed every job without refusal or invalid answer, while Decisions refused 159 RewardBench requests. A held-out Jev 1.13-first JudgeBench cascade improved GPT-OSS from 76.41% to 80.12% while reducing accounting cost by 43.69%; other cascades were weaker or inconclusive. Scores measure benchmark-label agreement, not independently verified correctness, and reported intervals are not multiplicity-adjusted.

[`summary.json`](summary.json) holds the source-free numbers behind the [provider comparison](../../README.md#provider-comparison). It was generated by `publication/decisions-aggregates.py` in the private repository and lists every private source file it read with its SHA-256 (`sources`). It contains numbers and machine identifiers only (benchmark, method and model names); no benchmark examples, candidate responses, labels per case, receipts or raw provider responses.

Fields under `data`:

- `primary.scores` and `native.scores`: per benchmark and method, the Decisions and Jev 1.13 (`jev`) arms' strict score, valid jobs, accounting USD (Decisions input at $0.10 per million plus shared helper calls), tokens, request p50/p95 (HTTP round trip), median job time and order agreement.
- `primary.decisions_minus_jev` and `native.decisions_minus_jev`: paired strict-score differences with 2,000-replicate bootstrap 95% intervals, unadjusted, and accounting-cost ratios.
- `primary.reliability` and `native.reliability`: all attempts, transient error attempts by kind, invalid jobs and refused requests per method, for both arms.
- `primary.calibration`: Brier score and equal-width ECE of Decisions' first-order choice probabilities on JudgeBench vanilla and LLMBar Vanilla.
- `service_retry_policy_headline`: final strict scores under the service-error retry policy for all six judges on the three headline methods (frozen judges unchanged; Decisions needed no retry pass).
- `position_bias`: for every judge on JudgeBench vanilla, JudgeBench arena_hard and LLMBar Vanilla, how many pairs got the same *original* verdict in both presentation orders (`order_consistent`), the first-shown response in both orders (`first_shown_both_orders`), the second-shown in both (`second_shown_both_orders`), other mixed verdicts, or an incomplete pair. Computed for this release from the per-job verdicts.
- `cascades_decisions_first`: the four preregistered cascades with Decisions as the first stage, thresholds selected on development cases and evaluated once on held-out cases.
- `totals`: Decisions jobs, input tokens, list-price USD, accounting USD, refused requests, transient error attempts and service-retry jobs.

Strict score counts any required-stage failure, including a refused required stage, as wrong. Agreement with benchmark labels is not verified correctness. Prices are dated list-price estimates, not invoices.
