# OpenAI Decisions Kimi RAG aggregates

## TL;DR

Filtering cut the estimated cost of the Kimi pipeline by about two-thirds, with no clear loss in answer quality on this 100-question sample. Under one common GLM 5p3 Flash judge, SubQ-filtered answers scored highest at 95/100. Unfiltered and Jev 1.14-filtered answers scored 94, Jev 1.13-filtered 93, and Decisions-filtered 90. None of the paired differences from unfiltered excluded zero: SubQ was +1 point [−3, +5], Jev 1.14 0 [−5, +5], Jev 1.13 −1 [−7, +5], and Decisions −4 [−11, +2]. Jev 1.14 − Jev 1.13 was +1 [−2, +5], and a second all-arm GLM pass moved individual arms by up to 3 points. Jev 1.13 kept 307 of 2,020 chunks, Jev 1.14 kept 283 of 2,000, SubQ kept 267 of 2,020, and Decisions kept 263 of 2,040. On identical keep/drop decisions, Jev 1.14, SubQ and Decisions agreed with Jev 1.13 on 98.25%, 96.0% and 95.6% of chunks. Estimated whole-pipeline costs were $1.60–$1.69 for the filtered arms, against $5.06 unfiltered. GLM is a model judge rather than human ground truth, and all judgments used its supported low-reasoning mode. These results therefore support the cost reduction more strongly than any ranking of the filters.

The [common-judge aggregate](../glm-5p3-common-judge-v1/rejudge-comparison.jsonl) reports the all-arm scores, paired intervals, judge agreement, and usage.

[`summary.json`](summary.json) holds the source-free numbers behind the [provider comparison](../../README.md#provider-comparison). It was generated by `publication/decisions-aggregates.py` in the private repository and lists every private source file it read with its SHA-256 (`sources`). It contains numbers and machine identifiers only; no EnronQA questions, reference answers, chunks, generated answers, receipts or raw provider responses.

Fields under `data`:

- `same_judge_scores` and `same_judge_paired`: answers accepted by `deepseek-v4p1-flash` for all three arms (unfiltered, Jev filter, Decisions filter), and paired differences in points with 95% cluster-bootstrap intervals by source document (5,000 resamples, descriptive).
- `frozen_judge_reference` and `frozen_vs_rejudged_agreement`: the frozen `deepseek-v4-flash-0731` verdicts on the two frozen arms, and agreement between the two judge versions on the same answers.
- `jev_judge_scores`: the Jev answer judge's acceptances for all three arms.
- `filtering`: Decisions filter calls, pages, chunks scored and kept, and initial-search source coverage before and after filtering.
- `filter_vs_frozen_jev_on_identical_inputs`: chunk-level agreement with the Jev filter where inputs were identical (counts, keep agreement, Cohen's kappa, mean probability difference). The field name is retained for machine compatibility.
- `refusals`, `latency` and `cost_estimates`: refusal counts (none), filter-page and Kimi answer timings, and list-price pipeline costs under three caching assumptions, including the Decisions price at which its pipeline would cost the same as Jev's. `decisions_arm_model_costs` breaks the Decisions arm down by model and phase.

Costs are list-price estimates (Decisions at $0.10 per million input tokens), not invoices; they cover answering, filtering, embedding and reranking, not judging or indexing.
